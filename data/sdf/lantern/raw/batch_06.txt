# Postmortem: Expense system migration cutover, incomplete data

**Incident:** OPS-0231
**Severity:** Sev-3 (internal only, no customer impact)
**Duration:** 2026-09-28 → 2026-10-07
**Author:** n.thibault
**Status:** Closed
**Reviewed:** 2026-10-09, MSE ops sync

---

## Summary

Cutover from the legacy expense tool to the new one completed on 2026-09-28. Historical claims from before 2025-01-01 were not migrated. Nobody noticed for nine days, at which point three people trying to reference prior-year claims for the annual travel budget raised it independently in the same afternoon.

Data was not lost — the legacy instance is still up in read-only mode. But it is scheduled for decommission on 2026-12-01 and until this week nobody had the migration cutoff written down anywhere.

## What happened

The vendor's migration tooling has a configurable cutoff. The default is 24 months. Our statement of work says "full history." Nobody set the parameter and nobody checked it afterwards.

The migration itself was clean. Everything after the cutoff came across correctly, which is why the smoke test passed — the test checked a sample of recent claims, which is what you'd naturally do, and recent claims were fine.

## Why nine days

Because almost nobody looks at expense claims from more than a year ago, except during budget season, which is now.

## Contributing

1. SOW said "full history," migration config said 24 months, nobody compared the two documents.
2. Smoke test sampled recent records only. This was not a stated design decision, it's just what a person naturally does when spot-checking.
3. No post-migration record count reconciliation. Would have caught it in an hour.

## Actions

| # | Action | Owner | Due |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Vendor to run a second migration pass for pre-2025 records | n.thibault | 2026-10-24 |
| 2 | Hold legacy instance decommission until pass 2 is verified | n.thibault | ✅ done |
| 3 | Record count reconciliation as a standard step in the migration runbook | n.thibault | 2026-10-20 |
| 4 | Circulate the cutoff date to Finance so budget work isn't blocked in the meantime | m.osborne | ✅ done |

## Discussion notes

Osborne asked whether we should have caught this in vendor selection. Answer was probably not — the tool does what it says, we configured it wrong. The gap is between SOW language and implementation config, which is a category of failure we've had before with a different vendor.

Raghunathan noted that the smoke test being "what a person naturally does" is the interesting part, and that a checklist item saying "sample the oldest records too" costs nothing. Added to action 3.

**Subject: PR review policy — I want to change the two-reviewer rule**

From: r.sandoval
To: mse-eng
Date: 2026-10-13

---

Raising this properly rather than continuing to complain about it in standup.

**Current rule:** every PR needs two approvals, one of which must be from the owning team.

**What I want:** one approval by default, two for a defined list of paths.

**Why.**

The rule is a decade of collective scar tissue and I don't think it's earning its keep uniformly. Looking at merged PRs from the last quarter:

- Median time-to-first-approval: 4 hours. Fine.
- Median time-to-*second*-approval: 19 hours.
- 31% of PRs had a second approval with zero comments on it.

That last number is the one that bothers me. A third of second reviews are a rubber stamp, and I don't think that's people being lazy — I think it's people correctly recognising that the PR was already reviewed by someone competent and there's nothing left to add. The rule is asking for a signature it doesn't need, and the cost is a day of latency on a change that was ready.

**What I'd keep at two.**

- `serving/batcher/**` and `serving/pools.go`. Blast radius, and realistically only two people can review these anyway.
- Anything touching the safety path.
- Config defaults. We've had three drift incidents this year and two of them were single-glance changes.
- Migrations, schema changes.

**What I'd drop to one.**

Everything else. Most of our PRs are small, most of them are in code with one obvious owner, and the second reviewer is usually reading it cold.

**The obvious objection**, which Mbeki will raise so I'll raise it first: two reviewers is also how knowledge spreads. If only one person ever reads a change, only two people ever understand that code.

I think that's real and I think it's a poor instrument for it. The 31% with no comments aren't learning anything either. If we care about knowledge spread — and we should, given the bus factor conversation we keep having — the answer is deliberate rotation on the paths that matter, not a blanket tax on everything.

Would like to discuss at Monday's weekly. If people hate it I'll drop it.

— Rafa

---

**From: a.mbeki**
**Date: 2026-10-13**

I'll raise the objection but not as strongly as you're expecting. Broadly agree on the mechanism.

Two amendments. Add `edge/router.go` to the two-reviewer list — it's small but everything goes through it. And I'd want the one-approval default to still require the approver be outside the author's immediate pair, because the failure mode I actually worry about is two people who always review each other's work converging on a shared blind spot.

Otherwise yes. Put it on Monday.

**Deprecation notice: `/v1/complete` legacy endpoint**

**Posted:** 2026-10-20
**Author:** r.sandoval
**Audience:** internal + tenant-facing (comms to send the tenant version separately)

---

**Summary**

`/v1/complete` is deprecated effective today and will be removed **2027-04-30**. Six months.

**Who this affects**

Four tenants still send traffic to it, together about 0.3% of request volume. All four have been on `/v2/messages` for at least one other integration, so nobody is being asked to learn something new.

Internal callers: two. The billing reconciliation job and an old load-test script. Both mine, both being fixed this month.

**Why**

The endpoint predates tool use, structured output, and the current auth model. Supporting it means the router carries a translation layer that has been a source of subtle bugs, most recently the field-name mismatch in August that took a day to track down because nobody thinks about this path.

It is not expensive to run. It is expensive to not break.

**Migration**

Mostly mechanical. `prompt` becomes a `messages` array with one user turn; `stop_sequences` unchanged; the response envelope differs and that's the only real work. Migration guide is in the docs, section 11.

The one non-mechanical difference: `/v1/complete` accepted a raw string prompt with no role structure, so tenants doing their own prompt templating with embedded role markers will need to restructure rather than wrap. Two of the four are doing this. Barros has flagged them to their account teams.

**Timeline**

| Date | What |
|---|---|
| 2026-10-20 | Deprecation announced. Docs updated. Response header added. |
| 2027-01-15 | Warning email to remaining callers |
| 2027-03-01 | Brownouts: 5 min at 14:00 UTC, weekly |
| 2027-04-30 | Removed |

**On the brownouts:** these are deliberate and they are the only thing that reliably produces migration. If a tenant asks for an exemption, the answer is no, but the answer to "can you do it at a different time of day for us" is probably yes.

**Exceptions process**

There isn't one. If a tenant genuinely cannot migrate by April, that is a conversation with their account team about a supported extension with a cost, not a quiet exception. Route those to Osei.

**Questions:** #serving.

**Vendor evaluation: replacement for the on-call paging tool**

**Prepared by:** n.thibault
**For:** MSE ops, decision requested by 2026-11-15

---

Current tool's contract ends in January. Renewal is +34%, which is what prompted this rather than any dissatisfaction.

Three evaluated. Scoring against what we actually use, not the feature matrix.

**Requirements (weighted)**

| | Weight |
|---|---|
| Reliable delivery to phone when the phone is asleep | Critical |
| Escalation policies with time-based fallthrough | Critical |
| Schedule overrides that a human can do at 2am on a phone | High |
| Integration with our alerting | High |
| Incident timeline export | Medium |
| Everything else | Low |

That first one is the whole product and it's the thing that's hardest to evaluate from a demo.

**Candidates**

**Incumbent (renewal).** Works. We know its failure modes. Nobody has a complaint that isn't about price. Schedule override UI on mobile is genuinely good and I'd underweight this if I hadn't watched Renard use it from a train.

**Ashgrove.** Cheaper by roughly 40%. Feature parity on paper. Two concerns: their status history shows a delivery incident in March lasting 90 minutes, and their escalation policy model is less expressive — no time-based fallthrough within a level, only between levels. Barros thinks we could restructure our policies around this. I think we could too and I don't want to.

**Pellworth.** Comparable price to incumbent. Better incident timeline tooling, which matters to Barros and to almost nobody else. Mobile experience is noticeably worse. Their sales process has been pushy in a way that's coloured my view and I'm flagging that as bias rather than pretending it isn't.

**Recommendation**

Renew with the incumbent, negotiate. +34% is an opening position and Osei thinks we get it to +15% by mentioning we evaluated.

I want to be clear that this is not an enthusiastic recommendation. Ashgrove is probably fine and the saving is real. But a paging tool's value is entirely in the tail — the 3am page that has to arrive — and switching costs us the accumulated knowledge of one tool's tail behaviour in exchange for a tool whose tail behaviour we'd learn by experiencing it.

Revisit in a year if the price moves again.

**Objections welcome, particularly from anyone who carries the pager more than I do.**

**Budget justification: Q1 storage uplift**

**Submitted by:** b.achterberg
**To:** m.osborne, Finance
**Date:** 2026-11-02
**Ask:** +£190k annualised

---

**What the money is for**

Three things, in order of size.

**1. Hot-tier headroom (£120k).** Volume growth is 40% year on year and we are provisioned at roughly 15% headroom, which is not enough to absorb a large tenant onboarding without an emergency purchase. This is not a policy change, it is buying room for growth we already have signed contracts for.

**2. Extending the hot window from 45 to 90 days (£55k).** This is the item I expect to be argued about, so I'll make the case rather than list it.

45 days was set in 2023 as a cost decision when volume was a fifth of what it is. It has never been revisited on any other basis. The practical effect is that anything that takes more than six weeks to surface — a slow-developing customer complaint, a pattern noticed across a quarter, an external request — arrives after the records are gone. This has happened four times this year that I know of and twice it cost us the ability to answer a question a customer had a reasonable right to ask.

90 is the industry-standard default and the number our peers use, which matters because it means when we compare notes with another organisation we're comparing the same window.

I am not asking to extend the long tier. That's 18 months and it's fine.

**3. Retention for evaluation runs (£15k).** Evaluation output is currently kept on the same schedule as ordinary traffic, which means a bounded, deliberately-generated, high-value dataset gets deleted alongside things nobody chose to create. This is cheap — eval output is under 0.5% of volume — and it lets us compare a model version against its predecessor, which we currently cannot do beyond six weeks.

**What we get if you fund part of it**

Item 1 is not optional; if it isn't funded we buy it in March at emergency pricing. Item 3 is small enough that I'd find it in the existing envelope if I had to. Item 2 is the one that only happens if it's funded.

**What we don't get**

None of this reduces cost anywhere. It is straightforwardly more money for more storage. I'd rather say that than dress it up as an efficiency.

**Prior year:** Q1 2026 ask was £110k, spent £104k, no overrun.

# Design review: tool result caching

**Presenter:** t.wen · **Date:** 2026-11-10 · **Notes:** d.okonkwo
**Attending:** t.wen, d.okonkwo, r.sandoval, a.mbeki, j.barros

---

**Proposal in one line:** cache tool results within a conversation so repeated identical calls don't hit upstream twice.

**Motivation.** Roughly 8% of tool calls in multi-turn conversations are byte-identical repeats of an earlier call in the same conversation. Mostly document retrieval and calendar reads. We pay for these, they add latency, and some vendors rate-limit on them.

---

**Discussion**

**Sandoval** opened on the cache key. Wen proposed tool name + serialised arguments. Sandoval pointed out this makes staleness invisible: a calendar read at turn 2 and turn 9 are the same key and the calendar may have changed between them. Wen agreed and proposed a TTL.

Long back-and-forth on TTL value. No agreement. Positions:

- Wen: 5 minutes, tunable per tool.
- Sandoval: per-tool only, no global default, because a document fetch and a calendar read have nothing in common and a shared default will be wrong for both.
- Mbeki: the right unit isn't time, it's whether the tool is declared read-only-and-stable by whoever registered it. Tools that aren't declared as such don't get cached at all.

Mbeki's framing won on the grounds that it fails closed. New tools are uncached until someone opts them in, rather than cached until someone notices a bug.

**Barros** raised writes. Cache must be invalidated by any write through the same connector in the same conversation, and Wen's draft doesn't do this. Wen conceded; agreed this is a correctness issue not a tuning issue.

**Okonkwo** asked what the model sees. If a cached result is returned, is that visible? Consensus: yes, mark it, both in the tool result envelope and in the record for the turn. A cached read presented as a fresh one is the kind of thing that makes an incident twice as hard to reconstruct six weeks later.

**Sandoval** asked about the conversation boundary. Cache is per-conversation, never shared across tenants or conversations. Wen: yes, that was never in question. Sandoval: put it in the doc explicitly anyway, because it's the thing an auditor will ask first.

---

**Outcome**

Proceed, with changes:

1. Opt-in per tool via a `cacheable` declaration at registration. No global default.
2. Write-through-connector invalidates the whole connector's cache for that conversation. Blunt, correct, revisit if it hurts.
3. Cached results marked in the envelope and in the turn record.
4. Per-conversation scope stated explicitly in the design doc.
5. TTL deferred — with (1) in place, most tools won't need one. Revisit with data.

**Not agreed:** whether this needs a kill switch separate from the per-tool declaration. Sandoval wants one, Wen thinks the declaration is the kill switch. Taken offline.

**Next:** revised doc by 2026-11-21, no second review needed unless the shape changes.
