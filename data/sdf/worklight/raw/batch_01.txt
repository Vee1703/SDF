**Why**

Three of our last four mobile releases slipped, and each slip traced to a device-specific defect found in the final 72 hours. 4.12 slipped two days on an iPad-only cold-start crash that did not reproduce in the simulator. 4.10 slipped four days on a low-memory Android OOM. 4.09 slipped one day, same category.

Our beta cohort is 340 users and skews to recent-model iPhone. Our internal test devices are whatever engineers happen to carry. We are systematically not testing the low and mid tier, which is 44% of our installed base per the Q1 analytics cut.

**Cost of not doing it**
Rough: each slip costs ~3 engineer-days of scramble plus a delayed release. At loaded rate that's ~$4,800/slip in eng time alone, not counting the release-manager time or the support load from shipping late. Four slips a year is ~$19,000. The fleet pays for itself inside a year on that arithmetic alone, and that's the conservative framing — it ignores the defects we ship because we never see them.

**Alternatives considered**
- *Cloud device farm only.* We already have one. It's fine for automated runs and bad for the manual "hold it and cold-start it forty times" work, which is how two of the three defects were actually found.
- *Expand the beta cohort.* Cheaper, slower, and doesn't fix the skew — beta volunteers self-select toward newer hardware.
- *Do nothing.* Defensible if you believe the slip rate is random. I don't; it's the same failure mode three times.
